Week 2: Creating Websites

In Week 2’s lecture, I explored the shift from early web networks to van Dijck’s platform society. Platforms organise social life through data‑driven algorithms, which are frequently treated as neutral, objective systems. However, the reading showed algorithms are socially shaped human creations. They can produce suppression of certain voices, prompting users to develop different forms of everyday resistance. I found the distinction between mathematical fairness and social fairness particularly thought‑provoking, as technical fixes alone cannot resolve deep‑rooted social inequalities.

In the workshop, I started constructing my portfolio website. I worked with CampusPress, wrote basic HTML/CSS and deployed files on GitHub Pages. This hands‑on practice helped me demystify web infrastructure. Instead of viewing digital platforms as abstract black boxes, I now understand they are tangible, built environments.

This practical task reinforces critical‑thinking principles from writing sessions. My portfolio will document both my practical outputs and theoretical reflections. Moving forward, I intend to consistently question platform power behind everyday digital experiences, rather than only describing what I observe online.
