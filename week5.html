Week 5: Data Practices

Week 5 built upon our week‑4 dataset about student generative‑AI usage, focusing on the critical practice of data visualisation. At the start of the group session, we reviewed our prior data‑building work. We acknowledged its weaknesses including limited sample size, subjective coding decisions and unavoidable data omissions, which deepened my understanding of how power operates within every stage of data work.

During the practical exercise, our group experimented with Excel for basic chart‑making and got an overview of Tableau Public. Before generating any graphics, we considered our target audience — university teaching staff — and clarified the core messages we hoped to communicate. I realised that chart‑type selection, colour choices and layout are not purely technical decisions; they actively frame narratives and can simplify complex social realities.

Following the module’s critical‑writing principles, I understand that visual outputs cannot fix flaws embedded in the original dataset. Even clean‑looking charts require careful human interpretation rather than being accepted as objective proof. I intend to add our unfinished visual drafts together with critical commentary to my portfolio. From now on, I will keep paying close attention to which voices get highlighted and which remain invisible whenever I create or read data‑driven visuals.
