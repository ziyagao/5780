Week 6: Identity, Algorithmic Identity and Representation

Week 6’s lecture unpacked the theory of algorithmic identity. Drawing on Cheney‑Lippold, I learnt that platforms construct our data‑doubles not merely from posted content, but from metadata, behavioural traces and network connections. Human identity is fluid, relational and context‑dependent, yet algorithmic systems convert these complex lived experiences into simplified categories designed for commercial objectives.

Within the workshop, I inspected my ad preference settings on a social‑media platform. Several algorithm‑generated interest labels failed to correspond with my real‑world self‑understanding. I also began Sumpter’s manual‑scraping exercise, assigning social‑media posts into fixed predefined categories. This hands‑on task demonstrated inherent limits: many posts resist single‑category labelling, and human subjective judgement inevitably shapes classification outcomes.

Following module critical‑thinking standards, I recognise algorithmic profiles serve platform logics rather than offering neutral self‑reflection. I will document observations from both the ad‑profile review and partial scraping work as portfolio evidence. Moving forward, I intend to consistently interrogate the disconnect between our lived identities and the simplified versions produced by digital algorithmic systems.
