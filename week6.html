Week 6: Identity, Algorithmic Identity and Representation

This session reframed online identity as an ongoing negotiation rather than a fixed self waiting to be displayed. Learning about optimisation loops and self-branding showed how platform metrics such as watch time and engagement can quietly reshape what feels desirable, visible or successful. Meanwhile, readings around metadata and data doubles made me think about how profiles and scores follow users into institutional spaces, even when interfaces promise personal control. I reflected on how daily habits and algorithmic feedback together construct the versions of ourselves we perform online.

This week I explored algorithmic identity through Rednote. In Task 1, I reviewed app privacy settings. Beyond posts I share myself, the platform collects behavioural data such as clicks and viewing time. It can only track digital actions, not my inner thoughts.

In Task 2, I examined my content preference chart. Some interest labels matched me, and I could edit these tags, but I cannot fully see the algorithm’s underlying decision-making process.
