Week 3: Web Scraping

In Week 3’s practical workshop, I learned about web scraping as a digital‑media research method. I experimented with the WebScraper.io browser extension and tested sample Python scripts in Google Colab to extract structured data from web pages. This hands‑on exercise demonstrated how researchers can gather large‑scale datasets directly from online HTML sources.

Importantly, I recognised that scraped data is not objective or complete. Scraping only captures information encoded within website code, filtering out contextual meaning, subjective human interpretation and unrecorded background details. Technical obstacles such as anti‑scraping restrictions also limit what we can collect. Additionally, the workshop pushed me to think ethically: public online access does not equal unrestricted permission for research data‑harvesting.

Following the module’s critical‑writing guidance, I understand raw scraped outputs still demand careful human analysis and evaluation. I will document my scraping attempt, including its difficulties, on my portfolio site as practical evidence. For future research, I intend to balance the utility of scraping against its methodological gaps and ethical risks, rather than over‑trusting data produced by automated tools.
