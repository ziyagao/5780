Week 7: Reflective Pause

This week’s session deepened my understanding of algorithmic identity through group‑based critical review of Sumpter’s manual coding experiment. Building on Cheney‑Lippold and Safiya Noble’s scholarship, I considered how algorithmic profiling not only represents users, but also shapes the information we access and can reproduce societal biases.

During our small‑group discussion, we interrogated the limitations of Sumpter’s classification method. The rigid preset categories struggle to capture multidimensional social‑media content. Human subjectivity inevitably influences how I assign posts to groups, and the limited sample of recent posts cannot fully represent a person’s complex online‑offline identity. If I repeated this practice, I would revise category definitions and add contextual notes to reduce oversimplification.

Following the module’s critical‑thinking framework, I drew comparisons between human manual coding and platform algorithms: both simplify messy social realities by applying fixed classification logics. I have added my group reflections and methodological critique to my portfolio to prepare for Assessment 1. Going forward, I will keep paying attention to how classification choices, whether human‑led or automated, produce incomplete versions of individual identity.
