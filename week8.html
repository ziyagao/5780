Week 8: Digital Ecologies

Studying media through digital ecologies helped me move past the clean, abstract idea of “the cloud” and “net zero”. Reading about mineral extraction, e-waste and rising water and energy use of data centres — including sites operating near Leeds — made computing’s physical footprint tangible. Practices such as data visceralisation and counter-mapping offer ways to move beyond abstract statistics and help people feel ecological harm. Moving forward, I want to be more intentional with my digital habits, including how often I turn to AI tools and how I manage my data and files.

This week’s workshop turned digital ecologies into a sensory, embodied practice at the Roger Stevens outdoor campus space. Observing through listening, smell and texture broke my usual reliance on sight and static maps. Slow sonic meditation and sensory trails revealed subtle more-than-human connections between weather, wildlife, buildings and the environment. Translating these observations into a sensory map and tracing actor-network links showed mediation shapes how we understand place, and how much detail is lost when complex systems are simplified into visual diagrams.
