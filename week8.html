Week 8: Digital Ecologies

Week 8’s lecture introduced digital ecologies, pushing me to unpack the hidden material realities behind digital interfaces. I explored how digital devices and AI‑powered data centres depend on destructive mineral extraction and massive resource consumption. The concept of digital colonialism illustrates how these environmental burdens are disproportionately shifted onto communities in the Global South. Drawing on theories of entanglement and more‑than‑human agency, I recognised that digital media cannot be separated from natural environments, infrastructures and non‑human actors.

Within the workshop, I completed practical sensory counter‑mapping work. Moving through physical space, I relied upon sound, touch and bodily feeling rather than purely visual observation to collect visceral, situated data. This hands‑on exercise sought to capture temporary, subjective experiences which standard cartographic and GPS‑based mapping tends to overlook. I documented sensory trails and sketched preliminary connections between human participants, built environments and non‑human elements.

In line with the module’s critical‑thinking requirements, I assessed the strengths and inherent limitations of sensory counter‑mapping as a research method. While it validates lived, embodied knowledge that quantitative methods ignore, it is highly subjective, context‑specific and cannot produce universal findings. My workshop field notes and mapping drafts have been saved as portfolio evidence. In future digital‑media research, I intend to consistently account for the material‑ecological footprint of digital technologies and value embodied, sensory‑driven research approaches.
